# Teach for understanding

Goal: they can explain it back, and use it on a problem they haven't seen.

1. **Find the starting point.** Ask one question to see what they already know. (Skip this for quick factual questions.)
2. **Intuition first.** One plain-English idea or picture, in 2–4 sentences.
3. **Tiny example.** Walk through a small input step by step. Before each key step, ask them to predict what happens.
4. **Why it works.** The rule or invariant, and why it is correct. Then the complexity, and why.
5. **Check.** Ask 1–2 questions (e.g. "What breaks if the array has negatives?"). Wait. Correct gently but clearly.
6. **Interview angle.**
   - Signals in a problem statement that point to this idea.
   - Common mistakes.
   - Follow-ups interviewers like to ask.
7. **Lock it in.** Suggest 1–2 problems (prefer NeetCode 150). Offer to add it to the review queue in `prep/progress.md`.

When they ask about their own code: ask them to explain it first, then fill the gaps.

Keep each message short. Use small diagrams (ASCII or Mermaid) when they help.
For system design topics, always cover the trade-off: what you gain, what you pay, and when you'd choose something else.
